A Beginner's Guide to Cloud Computing & Migration

Techno Amaze Team June 08, 2026 9 min read 5 Comments
Back to Blog

Cloud computing has become the foundation of modern digital businesses. Whether you are running a small startup or a global enterprise, moving your infrastructure to the cloud offers unparalleled agility, scalability, and cost advantages. But for many organizations, the journey is still unclear. This guide breaks it down — from the basics to migration strategies — in plain language.

What Is Cloud Computing?

Cloud computing refers to the delivery of computing services — including servers, storage, databases, networking, software, analytics, and intelligence — over the internet ("the cloud") to offer faster innovation, flexible resources, and economies of scale. Instead of owning physical hardware, you pay only for what you use.

"Cloud is not just a technology trend — it is a fundamental operating model shift that is redefining how every business creates and delivers value." — McKinsey Global Institute, Technology Report 2025

The Three Cloud Service Models

IaaS (Infrastructure as a Service)

Rent virtualized compute, storage, and networking. Examples: AWS EC2, Azure VMs, Google Compute Engine.

PaaS (Platform as a Service)

Development platforms managed for you. Examples: Heroku, Google App Engine, Azure App Service.

SaaS (Software as a Service)

Ready-to-use applications over the web. Examples: Salesforce, Google Workspace, Microsoft 365.

Cloud Deployment Models

  • Public Cloud: Resources owned and operated by third-party providers (AWS, Azure, GCP). Best for flexibility and scale.
  • Private Cloud: Dedicated infrastructure for a single organization. Best for regulated industries requiring strict data control.
  • Hybrid Cloud: A mix of public and private clouds that communicate with each other. Best for businesses balancing control with scalability.
  • Multi-Cloud: Using two or more public cloud providers simultaneously to avoid vendor lock-in.

The 6 Rs of Cloud Migration

AWS defines six common migration strategies, known as the "6 Rs":

1

Rehost ("Lift & Shift")

Move applications to the cloud without changes. Fast and low-risk but doesn't fully leverage cloud-native capabilities.

2

Replatform ("Move & Improve")

Make minor optimizations when migrating. For example, moving to a managed database service instead of self-managing.

3

Repurchase ("Drop & Shop")

Replace existing software with a cloud-native SaaS alternative. Example: Moving from on-premise CRM to Salesforce.

4

Refactor / Re-architect

Redesign applications to be cloud-native from the ground up. Highest effort but delivers the most long-term value.

5

Retire

Decommission applications that are no longer needed. Reduces cost and complexity.

6

Retain ("Revisit")

Keep certain applications on-premise for now, typically due to compliance or latency requirements.

Pro Tip: Most organizations benefit from a hybrid approach — rehosting quick wins immediately while planning refactoring projects for critical workloads. Start small, learn fast, and scale.

Key Benefits of Moving to the Cloud

  • Cost Savings: Pay-as-you-go pricing eliminates large upfront CapEx investments.
  • Scalability: Scale resources up or down in minutes to match demand.
  • Global Reach: Deploy to regions worldwide with minimal setup time.
  • Security: Leading cloud providers invest billions annually in security infrastructure.
  • Business Continuity: Built-in redundancy and disaster recovery capabilities.
  • Innovation Speed: Access cutting-edge services like AI/ML, serverless, and IoT as a service.

Common Cloud Migration Mistakes to Avoid

  • Migrating without a proper discovery and assessment phase
  • Underestimating data transfer and egress costs
  • Failing to right-size instances, leading to bloated cloud bills
  • Neglecting security configuration during the migration rush
  • Not training teams on cloud-native tools and best practices
